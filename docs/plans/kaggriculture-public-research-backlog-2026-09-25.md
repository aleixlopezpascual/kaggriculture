## What the medal target means

Kaggle's current progression page says that for competitions with at least 1,000 teams, bronze is top 10%, silver top 5%, and gold is top 10 plus an extra gold for every 500 teams; percentage cutoffs are rounded down.[3] On the 10,004-team public snapshot, that implies indicative cutoffs of **top 1,000 for bronze, top 500 for silver, and about top 30 for gold**.[3] Kaggriculture separately lists cash prizes for the top 10.[1] The final team count and final Bradley–Terry results can change, so these are targets and estimates, not guarantees.

In the historical 2026-09-25 snapshot, our recorded team position was outside the indicative bronze band by 351 places (rank 1,351 vs top 1,000). That made a bronze medal a concrete stretch objective; silver, gold, and a cash-prize finish are much more ambitious. The recorded 153.8-point score gap to the rank-1,000 score at that snapshot was an artifact for monitoring only—not a current live position or a reliable forecast of final rank.[25] The contest is still running and the final metric is pairwise results, so no probability of a medal can be inferred from that one snapshot.[1][3]

## Rules and strategy implications discovered

1. **The competition is decided by outcomes, not coin margin.** The official evaluation page says a win/loss/tie changes rating based on the result, not the coin difference; Kaggle says the final ranking will be a Bradley–Terry tournament after the submission deadline.[1] Kaggle staff clarified that ties count as half-wins, the team's final score is its better active submission, and the second slot is a hedge rather than a penalty.[5]
2. **Do not believe the old “final tournament wipes early history” note.** Kaggle staff clarified that the final fit uses all episodes between agents that remain active, and then clarified that **both** agents in an episode must remain active for that episode to count.[4] That conflicts with the claim in `docs/kaggle_discussions_summary.md` that the post-deadline tournament wipes early rating/age effects. Preserve the two strongest final-eligible agents; uploading a third replaces the oldest active submission and can retire useful evidence. The post-deadline play-rate increase is not guaranteed.[5]
3. **Recent public results show why opponent choice matters.** One earlier community tournament of 14 public implementations over 96 fresh seeds reported mostly chronological strength progression and no intransitive triple; a newer evaluation against a smaller set of strong public agents reports a clear three-agent win cycle.[6][8] These are author-run, different-cohort tests—not a contradiction to “average results”: they show that conclusions depend on which agents and lineages enter the panel.
4. **A strong local bank is not a medal predictor.** The recent `measure-first` notebook argues that a barely-trading/random opponent changes the economy, reports a large bank difference between an uncontested and ladder setting, and recommends paired boards, seats, reactive opponents, crash-screening, and win rate as the primary measure.[10] The `kaggle-environments` version in our repo and active virtual environment is already pinned to `1.32.7`, the same version used in that author's exact-replay check (`requirements.txt`, `docs/experiments/agent_selection/candidates.json`). However, `src/arena/evaluator.py::LocalArena.run_match` explicitly pits the agent against a PASS-only dummy; that is useful as a cash/debug diagnostic, not a competitive strength estimate.
5. **The leaderboard is a moving, copied meta.** The community reports public route/replay families spreading and then fading as stronger versions appear; it also documents how to replay an episode exactly and warns about step indexing and attribution.[8][9] This argues for a fresh opponent panel and for using replayed actions as measured controls or a backbone with state-aware recovery—not treating one stale tape or high page score as a durable champion.
6. **A pure replay clone is not a safe medal strategy.** Kaggle's rules set license, originality and winner-reproducibility requirements; the replay discussion explicitly recommends using a credited ancestor plus a substantive original decision layer rather than submitting a copied tape as the agent.[2][9] Use recorded play as a control, or as a documented backbone with our own state-aware policy and required notices.

One community PPO report describes reaching about $80k terminal cash and then stalling, but that is anecdotal rather than a proof that RL cannot work.[24]

Find one genuine delta, preserve notices, and test it alone.[14][20][21]
- **Do not tune on starter/random-only cash, one seed, one seat, or historical public rating.** These can reward an uncontested market, route memorization or matchmaking luck rather than a stronger final win probability.[6][7][10]
- **Do not upload merely to “reroll” a live rating.** It costs an active slot; current standings are not final evidence, and exact final scoring depends on active-agent pair histories.[4][5][7]
